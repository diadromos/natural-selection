Natural Selection: Camouflage (Spots)


White moths carry two genes: a spot shade (32-bit base gray; each spot varies around it by an offset set by the pattern gene) and a spot pattern (32-bit seed for spot count, sizes and positions; each spot center is redrawn until it lands on the moth). The predator eats the 8 whose area-weighted average gray, over wings and body, differs most from the background; 8 more die at random; each child takes the shade of one parent and the pattern of the other. Change the background any time with the slider.
